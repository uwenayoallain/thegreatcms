At five the hill is still blue. The first sound is never a car. It is a broom: somebody's long-handled _umweyo_ moving the night's dust off a compound, then the next compound, then the road.

I started waking at this hour by accident, during a year when sleep would not stay. Now I keep it on purpose. It is the only hour in the city that does not ask anything of me.

## Who is already up

The bakery on the corner has been lit for an hour. Women walk down from Kimihurura with baskets balanced so well that their hands are free to wave. A moto driver sleeps sitting upright on his bike, helmet hooked over the mirror, waiting for the first fare.

None of them is in a hurry, and none of them is idle. That combination is rarer than it sounds.

## What the hour is for

I do not write in it. I tried, and the sentences came out bright and thin. Instead I walk, and I notice, and by seven I know what the day's paragraph is about.

The essay you are reading was mostly decided on a single walk past the bakery, in the time it took the light to reach the bottom of the hill.
