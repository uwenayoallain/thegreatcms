At my mother's table, a sentence can begin in Kinyarwanda, borrow a French noun, and end with an English shrug. Visitors find it chaotic. I used to apologise for it.

## Choosing the word that fits

Then I started listening to _when_ we switch. My uncle moves into French for anything to do with paperwork, because that is the language the forms were in. My cousins reach for English when they are joking, because that is the language of the films they quote. And everyone returns to Kinyarwanda for anything that matters: a death, a wedding, a debt.

The switching is not a failure to choose. It is choosing, constantly, the language that holds the thing best.

## What translation taught me

Translating poetry made this obvious. Some words simply do not travel. _Agaciro_ is dignity, and worth, and value, and none of those. You do not translate it; you build a small room around it in the other language and invite the reader in.

My family does that at dinner, without thinking, every night.
